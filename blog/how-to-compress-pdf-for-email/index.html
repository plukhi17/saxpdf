How to compress a PDF for email

Use compression to reduce a PDF's size, then verify that the remaining text and images are clear enough to use.

SAXPDF Editorial · 22 November 2026 · 5 min read

Start with the attachment limit

Before processing, find out the maximum attachment size allowed by the sender and recipient mail systems. Many services use limits around 10–25 MB, but policies differ. A link or approved file-sharing service may be more appropriate for very large documents.

How to compress a PDF

  1. Open the Compress PDF tool. Choose the PDF you want to send.
  2. Choose a quality level. Start with Recommended for general use. Use a higher quality setting when fine text or detailed diagrams are important.
  3. Process and compare. Download the result and open it in a PDF viewer. Compare the size, readability, and key pages with the original.

What changes during compression?

The current SAXPDF compression workflow can rasterize pages to reduce image data. This may produce a smaller file, but text may stop being selectable and searchable. Interactive fields and other PDF features may not be preserved. Keep the original when you need an editable or accessible source file.

Choose the right target

If an email limit is 10 MB, do not stop at 10.9 MB if the recipient may forward the message. Leave a little margin for encoding and attachments. If compression cannot reach the required size, split the document or use a secure file-sharing service.

Frequently asked questions

What PDF size is safe for email?

Check the provider limit. Some systems accept up to 25 MB, while others accept less. A 10 MB target is a practical starting point for broad compatibility.

Does compression reduce PDF quality?

Yes, depending on the setting. Always inspect image detail and small text before sending.

What should I check after compression?

Verify file size, readability, page count, and the appearance of signatures or diagrams.

Need a smaller PDF?

Try a quality level and compare the result with your original.
